## Definition

A groupoid is a structure that categorizes equality. Not all equalities are equally equal : two equilateral triangles are "more" equal as triangles as compared to an equilateral and a scalene triangle. This study is best described inductively beginning from the tautologically existing concept : everything is equal. There can be no elements in such a concept because an element's essence only emerges in its difference of *some* kind from another element. Mathematically, we can call it the singleton set with the empty element $\{\varnothing\}$ .

Next we have the notion of strict inequalities. Things in a world which only has strict inequalities lack any nuance. Things in this concept have only one thing to care about : not equal to other things. Such a world is called a set in math. Elements of a set have no internal structure besides their one defining property: of being different from other elements. So there is no notion of some elements being more or less equal than others. It is a category of objects where two objects are "equal" if there is an arrow $\xrightarrow{=}$ between them, and not equal otherwise. This arrow is naturally symmetric, so if $x \xrightarrow{=} y$ then $y \xrightarrow{=}x$ . It is also transitive and reflexive. This is what we call a 0-groupoid.

Let us try to use this concept to describe the phenomena of set theory. Two sets in a set theory are equal if they are literally the same set. That is, $\{a,b,c\}$ and $\{a,b,c\}$ are equal sets. Our 0-groupoid structure has no trouble describing this. The trouble however comes from an empirical need: Why does it matter what I actually write to describe the elements, mathematically? The answer is that it doesn't. If I use the set $\{a,b,c\}$ to prove a set theoretic statement (for example, there are exactly 6 ways to pick 2 fruits out of a bag of 3 different fruits), then I can very well replace it with $\{1,2,3\}$ to prove the exact same statement. This implies that there is something inside these two *different* (in our limited 0-groupoid worldview) objects that makes them sort of interchangeable for my purposes. This sort of equivalence is what mathematicians called an *isomorphism*. Naturally, we need a way to keep track of these new equivalences. We introduce a new type of arrow : "$\xrightarrow{\cong}$" to mean that if there is this arrow between two objects, they are not "equal", i.e $x \xrightarrow{\cancel{ = }} y$ but isomorphic : $x \xrightarrow{\cong}y$ .

We call this concept with two kinds of arrows a 1-groupoid. The strict equality arrow we call a 0-morphism, and the isomorphism arrow we call a 1-morphism.

As you can imagine, putting numbers to these things would inevitably lead us to contemplate higher groupoids with higher morphisms. The study of set theory however isn't a sufficient playground to necessitate these higher structures. In a discrete universe described only using sets, we would never need a 2-groupoid.

We can see the failure of 1-groupoids most clearly when we now try to describe geometric objects, like topological spaces. A topological space is just the notion of "space", stripped of any notion of fixed distances and fixed shapes. The defining property of a "topological space" is its internal geometrical constraints, for example the number of holes it has.

## Hegel

*For us*, a 0-groupoid is akin to a set, its being-for-itself emerges from the mediation of objects in it with other objects which they recognized as their being-for-another. (they are by definition different from them, and to that difference it owes its existence). This forms its abstract being-in-itself identity. However, in the course of experience it encounters what it thinks is another being-in-itself and finds itself in it (another set of the same cardinality), It seeks a difference from this other abstract being-in-itself and loses its being-for-itself in the process, for in the process of it asserting its independence, it makes itself a being-for-another. This leads its being-in-itself abstraction to gain a new multitude, and then it tries to mediate this being-for-another character (via forming an isomorphism map). What forms thus is the sublated notion of sets in a 1-groupoid. This structure is the immanent necessity of this mediation. The object now finds its being-for-itself as a category of sets with mediation within its constituent parts as determinate isomorphisms. This whole process again repeats when logic leads it to find another being-for-itself and finds itself within it. Its constituent determinate isomorphisms now again become immediate in relation with the constituent determinate isomorphisms of this other being-for-itself, and now it has gained a new being-for-another identity. Again a mediation opens up between these negated isomorphisms, which finally take the determinate form of 2-morphisms. This keeps repeating.

It's not that a being-in-itself accidentally runs into another like itself, but that the assertion of it being-in-itself posits the existence of a difference, that it is different from everything else and owes its existence to that difference, thus making it being-for-another.

When asked "Does a tree falling in a forest make a sound if nobody is there to hear it?", A simple consciousness would say "Yes it does, because sound waves are necessary for physics to work", while a self-consciousness would say "There is no immediate being of “sound”; there is no immediate being of “pressure waves” either; what exists is a lawful, mediated, gauge-invariant physical process, and calling that process “sound” already presupposes a relation to a subject."

Just like for us, a 1-groupoid is the sublated being-for-itself that mediates between a 0-groupoid set and its being-for-another 0-groupoid counterpart with the same cardinality, for *us*,  self-consciousness is the sublated being-for-itself that mediates between an objective in-itself and its being-for-consciousness.

The 1-groupoid is a being-for-itself for us, and similarly the self-consciousness is a being-for-itself for *us* but by *us* we refer exactly to this self-consciousness, and thus the second sublation is exactly the being-for-itself *of* *us*- the self-consciousness.

The attempt of this being-for-itself self-consciousness at determinacy that asserts its existence by forcing the negative self-consciousness to recognize itself as being-for-another, it becomes the master to this negative slave. It forces the slave to dress up as the master, to show how the slave's in-itself is nothing but a farce. However, in such an assertion it inadvertently becomes being-for-another itself, for the master's being-in-itself now depends on this obeyance.

*For us*, lordship is an arrow that runs only one way: the master's morphism onto the slave has no inverse, for the master refuses to find himself in the slave. Such a structure is not yet a groupoid but merely a category, a mediation that has not yet become equal to itself. Mutual recognition is precisely the invertibility of this arrow, where each self-consciousness finds itself in the other as the other finds itself in it, and "they recognize themselves as mutually recognizing one another". Only then is the one-sided mediation sublated into an isomorphism. The groupoid is thus the determinate form of mutual recognition: just as the 1-groupoid owes its being-for-itself to the invertibility of its isomorphisms, self-consciousness attains its being-for-itself only when its being-for-another is returned to it.

This applies at the level of a particular national-spirit believing itself to be an immediate being. It tries to coerce recognition of their being-in-itself national identity but refuses to participate in mutual recognition. However, this is unstable and a people that defines itself _through_ an enemy has not yet achieved freedom.
